Chapter 1
Executive Summary
The Problem
Students today have access to more educational information than any generation before them.
Thousands of universities.
Millions of courses.
Unlimited online content.
Career rankings.
Salary reports.
Influencers.
Social media.
Parents.
Teachers.
Counsellors.
Friends.
Artificial Intelligence.
Yet despite this abundance of information, students remain more uncertain than ever about one of the most important decisions of their lives:
What should I study, where should I study it, and why?
The problem is not a lack of information.
The problem is an inability to transform information into understanding.
Most educational guidance systems begin by asking students what they want to become.
Discovery begins by asking a fundamentally different question:
Who are you becoming?
________________________________________
The Vision
Project Discovery is a decision intelligence platform that empowers students to understand themselves before making educational decisions.
Rather than recommending careers based solely on aptitude tests or academic performance, Discovery builds an evolving understanding of each student through evidence-based reasoning.
It continuously aligns this evolving understanding with the world's educational knowledge to recommend educational pathways, universities, opportunities, and immediate next steps.
Discovery does not attempt to predict a student's future.
It enables students to make the next best decision using the best evidence currently available.
________________________________________
The Philosophy
Discovery is founded upon a simple belief:
Students should not chase careers.
They should chase excellence.
Excellence emerges when a student's interests, abilities, motivations, values, and aspirations are aligned with educational opportunities that allow them to flourish.
Commercial success often follows excellence.
Happiness often follows alignment.
Discovery therefore does not optimize for prestige.
It does not optimize for salary.
It does not optimize for rankings.
It optimizes for informed decision-making.
________________________________________
The Core Innovation
Traditional educational platforms compare students against predefined career templates.
Discovery introduces a fundamentally different approach.
It models two evolving systems:
1.	The world's educational ecosystem. 
2.	The student's evolving identity. 
The first is represented through an extensive Education Knowledge Graph.
The second is represented through an evolving Student Identity Graph.
Recommendations emerge through continuous alignment between these two graphs.
Every recommendation is transparent.
Every recommendation is explainable.
Every recommendation is supported by evidence.
________________________________________
What Discovery Does
Discovery helps students answer five progressively deeper questions.
Who am I?
Discovery helps students discover patterns within their interests, motivations, personality, learning preferences, values, and aspirations.
It never defines identity.
It continually improves its understanding of identity.
________________________________________
What can I do?
Discovery identifies capabilities, strengths, competencies, and areas where students naturally demonstrate potential.
Rather than measuring intelligence alone, Discovery attempts to understand how students learn, think, solve problems, and remain engaged.
________________________________________
Who can I become?
Identity is not fixed.
Students grow.
Their interests evolve.
Their ambitions change.
Discovery treats growth as evidence rather than inconsistency.
Recommendations evolve alongside the student.
________________________________________
What is possible?
Discovery models the educational world through universities, programs, opportunities, scholarships, competitions, pathways, and learning experiences.
Students are not shown only one destination.
They are shown multiple evidence-backed possibilities.
________________________________________
What should I do next?
Discovery focuses on immediate decisions rather than distant predictions.
The platform continuously recommends meaningful next steps appropriate to the student's current stage of development.
________________________________________
What Discovery Is Not
Discovery is not a career counselling platform.
Discovery is not a university ranking website.
Discovery is not a college consultancy.
Discovery is not a psychometric testing platform.
Discovery is not an AI chatbot.
Discovery does not replace parents.
Discovery does not replace teachers.
Discovery does not replace counsellors.
Discovery empowers students to have better conversations with each of them.
________________________________________
Intended Outcome
Every student should leave Discovery with:
•	A clearer understanding of themselves. 
•	A deeper understanding of their educational possibilities. 
•	Confidence in the decisions they make. 
•	Evidence supporting those decisions. 
•	A continuously evolving roadmap for future growth. 
Success is therefore not measured by whether students choose a particular university or career.
Success is measured by whether students make informed decisions that remain aligned with their evolving identity.

Chapter 3
The Problem We Are Solving
________________________________________
3.1 Introduction
Every generation faces important decisions that shape the trajectory of their lives. Few decisions, however, are as consequential—or as irreversible—as the educational choices students are expected to make during adolescence.
Between the ages of eleven and eighteen, students are asked to choose subjects, streams, extracurricular activities, entrance examinations, degree programs, universities, and ultimately career pathways. These decisions influence not only their educational opportunities but also their professional identity, financial wellbeing, social networks, and personal fulfilment.
Paradoxically, these decisions are often made at a stage when students possess the least amount of self-awareness and the smallest understanding of the opportunities available to them.
Discovery exists because this problem is not simply difficult—it is fundamentally misframed.
The education ecosystem assumes that if students are given sufficient information, they will make better decisions.
We believe the opposite.
Students do not need more information.
Students need better understanding.
________________________________________
3.2 The Information Paradox
Modern students have access to unprecedented amounts of educational information.
Within minutes they can discover:
•	thousands of universities 
•	millions of degree programs 
•	global university rankings 
•	salary reports 
•	admission statistics 
•	student reviews 
•	career videos 
•	AI-generated advice 
•	educational influencers 
•	online communities 
•	counselling services 
The internet has effectively eliminated the scarcity of information.
Yet uncertainty continues to grow.
This phenomenon forms the central paradox that Discovery seeks to solve.
Information abundance has created decision paralysis rather than decision confidence.
Students are not overwhelmed because information is unavailable.
They are overwhelmed because information lacks structure, relevance, context, and personalization.
Discovery transforms information into understanding.

Final
Traditional reports represent conclusions.

•	mental stress

"What career pays the most?"

Chapter 4
Product Philosophy
"Technology should not tell students what to become. It should help them discover who they are becoming."
________________________________________
4.1 Introduction
Every product is built upon a set of assumptions.
Some are explicit.
Most remain invisible.
These assumptions influence every design decision, every algorithm, every recommendation, and every interaction.
Discovery was not built because the world needed another educational platform.
It was built because we fundamentally disagree with how educational decisions are made today.
The philosophy of Discovery is therefore not an accessory to the product.
It is the product.
Every feature, every recommendation, every interaction, and every future evolution of the platform must be consistent with the principles defined in this chapter.
________________________________________
4.2 Education Should Begin With Self-Discovery
Modern education asks students to answer questions that they are not yet equipped to answer.
"What stream will you choose?"
"What degree do you want?"
"What career interests you?"
"What university should you attend?"
These questions assume the student already understands themselves.
In reality, many students are still discovering:
•	what excites them, 
•	how they learn, 
•	what motivates them, 
•	what environments help them thrive, 
•	what they value, 
•	and who they are becoming. 
Discovery reverses the traditional order.
Instead of beginning with decisions, it begins with understanding.
Self-discovery is not the outcome of the platform.
It is the prerequisite for meaningful decision-making.
________________________________________
4.3 Discovery Does Not Decide
Perhaps the most important philosophical principle of the platform is this:
Discovery never makes decisions for students.
Educational decisions belong to students.
Not algorithms.
Not parents.
Not teachers.
Not counsellors.
Not artificial intelligence.
Discovery provides clarity.
Students provide choice.
The platform should never attempt to persuade.
It should illuminate.
Every recommendation exists to improve decision quality rather than replace human judgment.
________________________________________
4.4 Discovery Does Not Define Identity
Identity cannot be assigned.
It cannot be measured once.
It cannot be summarized by a personality score.
Discovery therefore rejects deterministic models of identity.
Instead, Discovery maintains an evolving understanding of the student through evidence-backed reasoning.
Identity is never declared.
Identity is continuously discovered.
The platform should never tell a student:
"You are creative."
Instead, it should communicate:
"Based on the evidence currently available, you consistently demonstrate behaviours associated with creative problem-solving."
The distinction is subtle but essential.
Discovery describes patterns.
It does not define people.
________________________________________
4.5 Understanding Is More Valuable Than Information
The modern world has solved information scarcity.
Students can access:
•	university rankings, 
•	admission statistics, 
•	salary reports, 
•	course catalogs, 
•	scholarships, 
•	career videos, 
•	AI-generated advice, 
•	educational influencers, 
•	discussion forums, 
•	and expert opinions. 
Despite this abundance, uncertainty continues to increase.
Information alone does not reduce uncertainty.
Understanding does.
Discovery exists to transform disconnected information into coherent understanding.
The platform should therefore optimize for clarity rather than quantity.
A student overwhelmed by twenty recommendations is less empowered than a student who deeply understands three.
________________________________________
4.6 Excellence Before Success
Society often encourages students to optimize for external indicators of success.
Higher salaries.
Prestigious institutions.
Popular professions.
Social approval.
Discovery adopts a different philosophy.
Students should first pursue alignment between:
•	interests, 
•	aptitude, 
•	values, 
•	motivations, 
•	opportunities, 
•	and aspirations. 
Excellence naturally emerges when individuals spend years developing capabilities within domains that genuinely engage them.
Commercial success frequently follows excellence.
Happiness often follows alignment.
Therefore Discovery does not optimize for prestige.
It optimizes for fit.
________________________________________
4.7 Growth Is Evidence
Traditional educational systems reward consistency.
Discovery celebrates growth.
Students change.
Interests evolve.
Dreams mature.
New experiences reshape aspirations.
These changes should never be interpreted as mistakes.
They represent learning.
When Discovery updates its recommendations, it is not correcting previous errors.
It is incorporating new evidence.
Every transformation becomes part of the student's journey.
Growth is not inconsistency.
Growth is evidence.
________________________________________
4.8 Recommendations Are Hypotheses
No algorithm can perfectly predict a student's future.
Discovery acknowledges this reality.
Every recommendation represents a hypothesis supported by the strongest evidence currently available.
As new evidence emerges, hypotheses evolve.
This philosophy introduces intellectual humility into the platform.
Discovery does not claim certainty.
It claims transparency.
Every recommendation should answer four questions:
•	Why am I seeing this? 
•	What evidence supports this? 
•	What evidence contradicts this? 
•	What additional information could improve this recommendation? 
Trust emerges not from certainty.
Trust emerges from explainability.
________________________________________
4.9 The Student Owns Their Story
Discovery accumulates knowledge over years.
It observes patterns.
It records growth.
It identifies emerging interests.
It remembers aspirations.
Yet none of this belongs to Discovery.
It belongs to the student.
Students own their identity.
Students own their evidence.
Students own their journey.
Discovery is merely its steward.
The platform should therefore prioritize:
•	transparency, 
•	portability, 
•	privacy, 
•	consent, 
•	and student ownership 
over platform retention.
A student's educational identity should never become captive to a technology platform.
________________________________________
4.10 Neutrality Is Sacred
Discovery exists for students.
Its recommendations must remain independent of commercial incentives.
No university should be promoted because it pays.
No program should rank higher because of sponsorship.
No educational pathway should be artificially favoured.
Neutrality is not a feature.
It is the foundation of trust.
Once neutrality is compromised, Discovery ceases to fulfil its purpose.
Every recommendation must therefore be defensible using publicly available evidence and transparent reasoning.
________________________________________
4.11 Discovery Optimizes for the Next Best Decision
One of the greatest misconceptions in educational guidance is the belief that students must discover their final destination before beginning their journey.
Discovery rejects this assumption.
The future cannot be predicted with certainty.
The next step can often be chosen with confidence.
Therefore the platform focuses on immediate decisions.
Examples include:
•	selecting the next subject, 
•	exploring a new field, 
•	participating in a competition, 
•	learning a skill, 
•	applying for a scholarship, 
•	visiting a university, 
•	or beginning a project. 
The accumulation of good decisions creates meaningful long-term outcomes.
Discovery does not optimize for a career twenty years from now.
It optimizes for the next decision that moves the student toward greater alignment.
________________________________________
4.12 The Four Questions of Discovery
Every recommendation generated by Discovery ultimately seeks to reconcile four fundamental questions.
Who am I?
An evolving understanding of the student's identity.
What can I do?
The student's demonstrated and emerging capabilities.
Who can I become?
The student's aspirations, potential, and capacity for growth.
What is possible?
The educational opportunities available within the world.
Discovery creates value not by maximizing any one of these dimensions.
It creates value by continuously reconciling all four.
This reconciliation forms the philosophical core of the platform.
________________________________________
4.13 Closing Principle
Discovery is not a platform that tells students what to do.
It is a platform that helps students understand themselves well enough to decide for themselves.
Every future feature, algorithm, interface, and recommendation should be evaluated against this principle.
If it increases understanding, it belongs in Discovery.
If it merely increases information, it does not.

Chapter 6
What Discovery Is
________________________________________
6.1 Introduction
Project Discovery is an evidence-based decision intelligence platform designed to help students make informed educational decisions by first understanding themselves.
Unlike traditional educational guidance platforms that begin with careers, colleges, or rankings, Discovery begins with the student.
Every recommendation generated by the platform emerges from continuously reconciling the student's evolving identity with the world's educational knowledge.
Discovery is designed to support students throughout Grades 6–12, adapting its experience as the student matures, while maintaining a single evolving understanding of who they are becoming.
________________________________________
6.2 Discovery Is a Decision Intelligence Platform
Discovery does not exist to provide information.
Information already exists.
Discovery exists to help students make better decisions.
The platform continuously transforms raw educational information into contextual, personalized understanding.
Rather than answering:
"Which college is the best?"
Discovery answers:
"Which educational pathways are currently most aligned with this student's evolving identity, aspirations, capabilities, and constraints?"
Decision quality—not information quantity—is the primary objective of the platform.
________________________________________
6.3 Discovery Is a Self-Discovery Platform
Every meaningful educational decision begins with self-understanding.
Discovery therefore prioritizes understanding the student before evaluating educational opportunities.
The platform continuously develops an evolving understanding of:
•	interests 
•	personality 
•	learning preferences 
•	motivations 
•	aspirations 
•	strengths 
•	values 
•	constraints 
•	academic development 
•	extracurricular interests 
•	decision readiness 
This understanding is continuously refined as new evidence becomes available.
________________________________________
6.4 Discovery Is Evidence-Based
Discovery never produces recommendations based solely on assumptions.
Every recommendation must be supported by observable evidence.
Evidence may originate from:
•	Adaptive Discovery Assessment 
•	academic performance 
•	subject preferences 
•	interests 
•	aspirations 
•	projects 
•	competitions 
•	extracurricular activities 
•	behavioural interactions 
•	student reflections 
•	future verified evidence sources

Chapter 7
What Discovery Is Not
________________________________________
7.1 Introduction
Understanding what Discovery is requires an equally clear understanding of what Discovery is not.
The education technology ecosystem already contains thousands of products that solve specific problems well. Discovery does not seek to replace every tool in the ecosystem. Instead, it occupies a distinct position within it.
Discovery exists before the application process, before college selection, and before career planning. Its purpose is to help students build sufficient self-understanding to make those decisions with confidence.
The following principles define the boundaries of the platform.
________________________________________
7.2 Discovery Is Not a Career Counselling Platform
Discovery does not attempt to tell students what career they should pursue.
Careers are treated as directional indicators rather than destinations.
For example, Discovery may identify that a student's identity aligns strongly with scientific research, analytical thinking, and experimentation. This may support multiple future careers including biomedical research, biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, environmental science, or academia.
The platform does not encourage students to optimize for a single job title.
Instead, it helps students identify educational pathways that preserve future optionality.
The immediate objective is selecting the right educational program—not predicting a job thirty years into the future.
________________________________________
7.3 Discovery Is Not a University Ranking Website
Discovery does not rank universities.
There is no concept of "best university."
There is only:
The most suitable university for a particular student under a particular set of circumstances.
University recommendations consider numerous factors, including:
•	academic alignment 
•	learning environment 
•	financial constraints 
•	location preferences 
•	teaching philosophy 
•	research opportunities 
•	scholarships 
•	campus culture 
•	student aspirations 
Rankings may exist as one source of public information but never determine recommendations on their own.
________________________________________
7.4 Discovery Is Not a College Consultancy
Discovery does not provide admission consulting services.
It does not write application essays.
It does not complete application forms.
It does not promise admissions.
It does not offer personalized admission guarantees.
Instead, Discovery prepares students to become stronger applicants by helping them make better educational decisions over many years.
The platform empowers students before the application process begins.
________________________________________
7.5 Discovery Is Not an Advertising Platform
Discovery will never monetize attention through advertisements.
Students should never question whether a recommendation exists because it is genuinely appropriate or because it is commercially sponsored.
The platform therefore excludes:
•	banner advertisements 
•	sponsored recommendations 
•	promoted universities 
•	promoted degree programs 
•	affiliate marketing 
•	paid educational rankings 
Trust is more valuable than advertising revenue.
________________________________________
7.6 Discovery Is Not a Marketplace
Discovery is not designed to connect students with institutions for commercial transactions.
Universities cannot purchase visibility.
Programs cannot purchase recommendations.
Educational organizations cannot pay to improve their position within Discovery.
The recommendation engine operates independently of commercial interests.
________________________________________
7.7 Discovery Is Not an AI Chatbot
Artificial Intelligence exists within Discovery as an internal capability—not as a conversational personality.
Students do not interact with an AI assistant that offers opinions or speculative advice.
Instead, AI is used to:
•	adapt assessments, 
•	interpret evidence, 
•	strengthen identity modelling, 
•	improve recommendation quality, 
•	detect uncertainty, 
•	identify relationships within knowledge, 
•	and improve explainability. 
Every recommendation remains grounded in evidence rather than generated opinion.
The intelligence of Discovery should be visible through the quality of its reasoning, not through conversation.
________________________________________
7.8 Discovery Is Not a One-Time Assessment
Discovery is not a psychometric report that is generated once and forgotten.
Identity changes.
Evidence grows.
Interests evolve.
Goals mature.
Recommendations therefore continue evolving throughout the student's educational journey.
Discovery is designed as a long-term companion rather than a single assessment experience.
________________________________________
7.9 Discovery Is Not a Personality Test
The objective of Discovery is not to categorize students.
Students are never reduced to labels such as:
•	Introvert 
•	Extrovert 
•	Creative 
•	Analytical 
•	Leader 
•	Thinker 
While validated psychometric frameworks contribute evidence to the Identity Engine, they never define identity by themselves.
Identity emerges from multiple sources of evidence observed over time.
________________________________________
7.10 Discovery Is Not a Replacement for Parents
Parents play a critical role in educational decision-making.
Discovery does not attempt to replace parental guidance.
Instead, it enables more informed conversations.
When disagreements occur—for example between a student's aspirations and parental expectations—Discovery provides transparent evidence that both parties can examine objectively.
The platform facilitates dialogue rather than taking sides.
________________________________________
7.11 Discovery Is Not a Replacement for Teachers

•	collaboration

•	initiative 
•	resilience

Human judgment remains valuable.

Professional counsellors provide contextual guidance that extends beyond educational recommendations.
Discovery supports counsellors by enabling students to better understand themselves before entering counselling sessions.
Rather than replacing counsellors, Discovery enables conversations to begin at a higher level of understanding.
The platform empowers the student.
The counsellor remains a trusted human advisor.
________________________________________
7.13 Discovery Is Not Static
Neither the educational ecosystem nor student identity remains constant.
Discovery therefore rejects static reports.
Every recommendation represents the platform's best understanding at a given point in time.
As evidence changes:

Chapter 8
How Discovery Thinks
________________________________________
8.1 Introduction
Discovery is not a search engine.
It is not a recommendation engine.
It is not an expert system.
It is not a chatbot.
It is a reasoning system.
Unlike traditional educational platforms that retrieve information or rank results, Discovery continuously reasons about a student's evolving identity and the educational world in order to generate evidence-based recommendations.
This distinction is fundamental.
Discovery does not ask:
"Which college is best?"
It asks:
"Given everything currently understood about this student, what educational choices appear most aligned with who they are becoming?"
Thinking—not searching—is the core capability of the platform.
________________________________________
8.2 Four Fundamental Questions
Every meaningful educational decision can be reduced to four interconnected questions.
1. Who am I?
The student's current identity.
Not as a fixed description, but as an evolving understanding.
Identity includes:
•	interests 
•	motivations 
•	values 
•	personality 
•	learning preferences 
•	aspirations 
•	capabilities 
•	constraints 
•	confidence 
________________________________________
2. What can I do?
The student's demonstrated and emerging capabilities.
This includes both academic and non-academic strengths.
Examples include:
•	analytical reasoning 
•	communication 
•	persistence 
•	creativity 
•	collaboration 
•	leadership 
•	research ability 
•	spatial reasoning 
•	problem solving 
Capability is inferred through accumulated evidence rather than isolated assessments.
________________________________________
3. Who can I become?
Potential is not prediction.
Potential represents the range of educational and professional directions that remain realistically achievable given the student's identity, growth trajectory, and opportunities.
Discovery never narrows a student to a single future.
Instead, it continuously expands and refines possible futures.
________________________________________
4. What is possible?
The educational world is constantly evolving.
New universities emerge.
Programs change.
Industries transform.
Scholarships appear.
Research opportunities expand.
Discovery continuously models this external world independently of any individual student.
The educational ecosystem represents possibility.
The student's identity represents preference.
Discovery exists to align the two.
________________________________________
8.3 Discovery as Reconciliation
These four questions rarely point toward the same answer.
A student may:
•	love literature, 
•	excel at mathematics, 
•	aspire to entrepreneurship, 
•	and face financial constraints. 
Each represents a valid dimension of reality.
Discovery does not allow any single dimension to dominate.
Instead, it reconciles them.
The role of Discovery is not to optimize one variable.
Its role is to balance competing truths.
Recommendations emerge from this reconciliation.
________________________________________
8.4 Identity Is an Understanding
Traditional systems treat identity as data.
Discovery treats identity as understanding.
Data answers:
"What did the student say?"
Understanding asks:
"What patterns emerge across everything we know?"
Identity therefore becomes a living model that continuously improves as additional evidence becomes available.
Discovery never assumes it has reached complete understanding.
It simply becomes progressively more accurate.
________________________________________
8.5 Discovery Reasons in Relationships
Educational decisions cannot be understood in isolation.
Every concept exists within a network of relationships.
For example:
•	A subject develops certain competencies. 
•	Competencies support educational programs. 
•	Programs lead to future opportunities. 
•	Universities provide different learning environments. 
•	Learning environments suit different students. 
Similarly:
•	Interests influence motivation. 
•	Motivation affects engagement. 
•	Engagement strengthens capability. 
•	Capability expands opportunity. 
Discovery reasons across these relationships rather than evaluating isolated attributes.
________________________________________
8.6 Every Recommendation Is a Hypothesis
Discovery does not produce answers.
It produces hypotheses.
Each recommendation represents the platform's current best explanation of what appears most aligned with the student's evolving identity.
A recommendation is therefore composed of:
•	the conclusion, 
•	supporting evidence, 
•	opposing evidence, 
•	confidence, 
•	assumptions, 
•	unanswered questions, 
•	and recommended next actions. 
As understanding improves, hypotheses improve.
________________________________________
8.7 Learning Never Stops
Discovery has no concept of a "completed profile."
Every meaningful interaction contributes additional understanding.
Examples include:
•	completing assessments, 
•	exploring educational pathways, 
•	updating aspirations, 
•	recording achievements, 
•	changing interests, 
•	interacting with recommendations. 
Identity therefore becomes richer over time.
Recommendations become increasingly personalized.
Confidence gradually improves.
The platform grows alongside the student.
________________________________________
8.8 Summary
Discovery does not think in terms of careers, colleges, or scores.
It thinks in terms of relationships.
It continuously reconciles:
•	who the student is, 
•	what the student can do, 
•	who the student might become, 
•	and what the educational world makes possible. 
Everything else in Discovery—the Knowledge Graph, Identity Graph, Recommendation Engine, and Roadmap—exists to support this single process of evidence-based reasoning.

Chapter 15
The Discovery Assessment
________________________________________
15.1 Purpose
The Discovery Assessment is the primary mechanism through which the platform begins understanding a student.
It is not a traditional psychometric test.
It is an adaptive discovery process designed to gather high-quality evidence while remaining engaging, age-appropriate, and scientifically grounded.
The objective is not to produce a score.
The objective is to generate reliable evidence for the Identity Engine.
________________________________________
15.2 Design Principles
The assessment should feel like exploration rather than examination.
Students should never feel that there are "correct" answers.
Questions should be framed to reveal preferences, behaviours, motivations, and decision-making patterns instead of testing academic knowledge.
________________________________________
15.3 Adaptive Assessment
The assessment adapts continuously based on previous responses.
Rather than presenting every student with the same questionnaire, Discovery dynamically selects the next question to maximize information gain.
The adaptive engine should prioritize:
•	reducing uncertainty, 
•	resolving conflicting evidence, 
•	strengthening weak identity claims, 
•	minimizing unnecessary questions. 
________________________________________
15.4 Assessment Categories
The Discovery Assessment should combine multiple evidence-generating formats.
Examples include:
•	scenario-based decision questions, 
•	preference comparisons, 
•	interest rankings, 
•	confidence ratings, 
•	reflection prompts, 
•	situational judgement exercises, 
•	learning preference questions, 
•	aspiration prompts, 
•	value trade-off questions, 
•	personality indicators. 
Future versions may incorporate interactive mini-games where scientifically validated.
________________________________________
15.5 Grade Adaptation
The assessment experience changes by educational stage.
Explorer (Grades 6–8):
•	playful language, 
•	shorter interactions, 
•	visual prompts, 
•	curiosity-focused questions. 
Researcher (Grades 9–10):
•	increased depth, 
•	subject exploration, 
•	competency discovery, 
•	opportunity awareness. 
Decider (Grades 11–12):
•	educational priorities, 
•	programme preferences, 
•	university considerations, 
•	financial and geographic constraints, 
•	application readiness. 
________________________________________
15.6 Completion Philosophy
Students are never expected to "finish" Discovery.
The initial assessment establishes a starting point.
Subsequent micro-assessments throughout the student's journey continuously improve the Identity Graph.
Assessment is therefore continuous rather than episodic.
________________________________________
15.7 Success Criteria
The Discovery Assessment succeeds when it:
•	produces high-quality evidence, 
•	minimizes assessment fatigue, 
•	adapts intelligently, 
•	remains engaging, 
•	improves recommendation confidence, 
•	evolves alongside the student rather than becoming obsolete.

The roadmap should prioritize depth over breadth. Each version should strengthen Discovery's ability to understand students before expanding into new feature areas.
Every release should answer one question:
Does this make Discovery better at helping students understand themselves and make informed educational decisions?
Features that do not strengthen this objective should be deferred.

Information

Understanding

↓

Educational Intelligence

To become the world's most trusted educational decision-support system.

Chapter 34
Non-Functional Requirements
34.1 Purpose
While functional requirements describe what Discovery should do, non-functional requirements define how well it should do it.
Given the platform's educational responsibility, quality attributes are not implementation details—they are product requirements.
________________________________________
34.2 Reliability
Students should be able to depend on Discovery throughout their educational journey.
The platform should:
•	recover gracefully from failures, 
•	prevent data loss, 
•	maintain recommendation consistency, 
•	preserve historical information. 
The Identity Graph should never become corrupted due to partial updates.
________________________________________
34.3 Performance
The system should feel immediate.
Target expectations:
•	Initial page load under 2 seconds. 
•	Identity Dashboard under 1 second. 
•	Recommendation generation under 3 seconds. 
•	Opportunity search under 2 seconds. 
•	Graph queries under 500 ms for common operations. 
As the Knowledge Graph scales, caching and indexing strategies should evolve without affecting the user experience.
________________________________________
34.4 Scalability
Discovery should be designed to support:
•	millions of students, 
•	millions of educational entities, 
•	hundreds of millions of graph relationships, 
•	continuous recommendation updates. 
Scaling should occur horizontally wherever possible.
No component should become a single point of failure.
________________________________________
34.5 Security
Student information represents highly sensitive educational data.
The platform should implement:
•	encryption in transit, 
•	encryption at rest, 
•	secure authentication, 
•	audit logging, 
•	least-privilege access, 
•	regular security reviews. 
Educational identity should be protected with the same seriousness as financial information.
________________________________________
34.6 Explainability
Explainability is a non-functional requirement.
Every recommendation must remain traceable regardless of future algorithmic improvements.
The platform should always be capable of answering:
•	Why was this recommendation generated? 
•	Which evidence influenced it? 
•	Which Knowledge Graph relationships were traversed? 
Loss of explainability is considered a system failure.
________________________________________
34.7 Availability
Discovery should target high availability.
Educational planning often occurs during admission cycles where downtime has significant consequences.
Maintenance windows should be minimized.
________________________________________
34.8 Maintainability
Every subsystem should expose clear interfaces.
The following components should be independently replaceable:
•	Assessment Engine 
•	Identity Engine 
•	Recommendation Engine 
•	Knowledge Graph 
•	Opportunity Engine 
Future AI integration should not require rewriting application logic.
________________________________________
34.9 Accessibility
Accessibility is a product requirement, not an enhancement.
The platform should support:
•	keyboard navigation, 
•	screen readers, 
•	scalable typography, 
•	sufficient colour contrast, 
•	reduced-motion preferences. 
Students with disabilities should receive an equivalent Discovery experience.
________________________________________
34.10 Internationalisation
Discovery is designed for global expansion.
The architecture should support:
•	multiple languages, 
•	multiple educational systems, 
•	local curricula, 
•	regional admission processes, 
•	local opportunity datasets. 
Internationalisation should be designed into the architecture from the outset.
________________________________________
34.11 Ethical Requirements
The platform must remain:
•	commercially neutral, 
•	evidence-based, 
•	transparent, 
•	respectful of student autonomy. 
Recommendations should never be influenced by sponsorship, advertising, or institutional partnerships.

34.12 Success Criteria
The system succeeds when users trust Discovery not only because of what it recommends, but because of how reliably it behaves.

Chapter 35
Appendix
35.1 Purpose
This appendix provides foundational definitions and reference material used throughout the Product Requirements Document.
Its purpose is to establish a shared vocabulary for designers, engineers, researchers, product managers, and future contributors.
________________________________________
35.2 Glossary
Discovery
The educational decision-support platform described throughout this document.
________________________________________
Knowledge Graph
The structured representation of educational knowledge, including subjects, competencies, programmes, institutions, opportunities and their relationships.
________________________________________
Evidence
Immutable observations collected about a student.
Evidence is factual.
It is never inferred.
________________________________________
Identity Engine
The reasoning system responsible for transforming evidence into probabilistic identity claims.
________________________________________
Student Identity Graph
A dynamic network of claims representing Discovery's current understanding of a student.
Identity evolves continuously as new evidence becomes available.
________________________________________
Identity Claim
A probabilistic statement generated by the Identity Engine.
Example:
"The student currently demonstrates a strong preference for project-based learning."
Claims are always accompanied by confidence and supporting evidence.
________________________________________
Recommendation
An evidence-based educational suggestion produced through alignment between the Student Identity Graph and the Knowledge Graph.
Recommendations are never deterministic.
________________________________________
Opportunity
An educational experience capable of generating additional evidence while supporting student development.
Examples include:
•	competitions, 
•	hackathons, 
•	research programmes, 
•	scholarships, 
•	volunteering. 
________________________________________
Roadmap
A dynamically generated sequence of recommended actions designed to strengthen student understanding and educational readiness.
________________________________________
Decision Readiness
A measure of how prepared a student is to make educational decisions.
Decision Readiness is distinct from grade level and evolves independently.
________________________________________
Explainability
The ability of the platform to fully justify every recommendation by tracing it through identity claims, supporting evidence, and Knowledge Graph relationships.
________________________________________
35.3 Foundational Product Principles
The following principles should remain invariant across all future versions of Discovery:
1.	Discovery does not define students; it continuously improves its understanding of them. 
2.	Identity is inferred, never declared. 
3.	Evidence is immutable. 
4.	Knowledge is objective. 
5.	Recommendations are explainable. 
6.	Growth is preserved. 
7.	Students own their data. 
8.	Educational neutrality is non-negotiable. 
9.	Every recommendation should expand possibilities rather than restrict them. 
10.	Discovery exists to help students understand themselves before asking them to decide their future.
