# ADR-003: Knowledge Governance, Lifecycles, and Provenance

**Status**: Accepted  
**Date**: 2026-07-17

## Context
As RADAR transitions into an enterprise-grade platform, knowledge management must move from simple script-based extraction to strict, auditable **Knowledge Governance**. Up to Sprint 3, our focus was entirely on *promotion* (discovering and importing new terms). However, a complete knowledge system must also handle *retirement* (dormancy, deprecation, and merging) and *provenance* (the verifiable chain of evidence from high-level capabilities back to the raw source text) to maintain longitudinal precision and prevent technical debt.

## Decision
We establish a formal, deterministic **Knowledge Governance framework** to regulate ontologies, capability mappings, and candidates.

```
       [ Observed ]
            │
            ▼ (TF_MIN met)
       [ Qualified ]
            │
            ▼ (Promotion gates passed)
       [ Certified ]
            │
            ▼ (Human-in-the-loop merge)
        [ Active ]
            │
            ├────────────────────────┐
            ▼ (Dormancy / Low Conf)  ▼ (Alias Match / Dup)
      [ Deprecated ]              [ Merged ]
            │
            ▼ (Pruned)
       [ Archived ]
```

### 1. The Knowledge Lifecycle States
Every node (Technology, Capability, or Alias) inside RADAR is classified into one of the following states:
1. **Observed**: Surfaced noun phrase inside raw source text.
2. **Qualified**: Mined phrase meeting the minimum document frequency threshold ($C_{docs} \ge TF\_MIN$).
3. **Certified**: Candidate officially stamped by the `EvidenceCandidateEngine` for meeting all promotion gates.
4. **Active**: Permanently merged into the production ontologies.
5. **Deprecated**: Flagged for retirement due to dormancy or score decay.
6. **Archived**: Permanently removed from active queries and preserved in historical cold storage.

### 2. Deterministic Retirement Gates
Knowledge does not live forever. We enforce four deterministic retirement gates:
- **Dormancy Rule**: Any `Active` product or capability not observed in the last $N$ months (or $M$ consecutive scrapings) is automatically transitioned to `Deprecated` status.
- **Confidence Degradation**: If cumulative confidence scores for aliases fall below $80\%$, they are flagged for human-in-the-loop review.
- **Deduplication & Merging**: If an `Active` candidate matches a newly added canonical product name, it is merged as an alias, and its standalone history is marked as `Merged`.
- **Pruning Rule**: Deprecated nodes remain visible for exactly 1 validation cycle before being permanently migrated to `Archived`.

### 3. Append-Only Evidence & Versioning
To maintain an immutable audit trail, we prohibit in-place mutation of mined candidates. Every execution of `scripts/mine-unrecognized-tech.ts` produces a version-controlled, timestamped report. Historical observations are committed to the git branch, allowing developers to reconstruct ontology candidates from any historical date.

### 4. Capability Provenance Chains
In Sprint 4, we will introduce capability mapping. To avoid "hallucinated" human skills, every capability mapping must maintain a structural provenance chain back to its foundational tools:
$$\text{Capability} \xrightarrow{\text{Provenance}} \{\text{Technologies}\} \xrightarrow{\text{Evidence}} \{\text{Tier 1 Documents}\}$$
For example: `CRM Strategy` is derived from `[Salesforce, HubSpot, Microsoft Dynamics]`.

## Consequences
- **Zero-Bloat Ontologies**: Prevent old, obsolete technologies from permanently polluting the system.
- **Verifiable Explanations**: Recommendations are fully explainable back to raw Tier 1 document text.
- **Auditability**: Promoted and retired terms can be audited chronologically.
